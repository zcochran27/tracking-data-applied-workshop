# Methodology and reproduction boundaries

## Data
SkillCorner match 1925299: Brisbane Roar FC vs Perth Glory, 21 December 2024. Tracking is broadcast-derived and includes extrapolated positions. The downloader caches the first 9000 JSONL records plus full metadata, dynamic events and phases. A bounded sample is not a complete match. Source data remain outside Git; rerun the downloader for execution. Credit SkillCorner and PySport. See the upstream repository's MIT license and documentation.

Use the original frame IDs and period IDs. Tracking uses native metric coordinates; dynamic event coordinates may be attack-normalized. Do not draw event coordinates on tracking without checking direction and dimensions. Phase joins use frame intervals within period and avoid the coordinate issue.

## Physical metrics
Compute derivatives separately within contiguous valid player/period segments. Smooth positions with a Gaussian kernel (sigma 1.5 frames); exclude three-sigma boundary support before estimating speed. These are explicit teaching choices, not SkillCorner's production filters. Derive speed in m/s then multiply by 3.6. Distance integrates speed over native 0.1-second intervals. Report valid seconds and sample coverage. Extrapolated coordinates can remain in these estimates; coordinate coverage is not camera-observed coverage.

The documented men's bands are HSR 20–25 km/h and sprint above 25 km/h. Our half-open code uses [20,25), [25,infinity) to avoid boundary double counting. Effort examples require 1 continuous second and do not merge gaps. Exact production effort rules are not asserted. Threshold sensitivity is interactive. No PSV-99 reconstruction is claimed: SkillCorner defines its activity population above 15 km/h, which differs from all raw frame speeds. Peak Velocity is distinct. Time-to-HSR/sprint requires a qualified start rule and is discussed rather than reconstructed. Acceleration vector magnitude and the signed derivative of scalar speed answer different questions.

## Pitch control
Voronoi assigns each grid point to its nearest player. Gaussian influence uses projected means, directional covariance and a logistic transformation of team influence differences. It illustrates the method family, not the exact Wide Open Spaces parameterization. The score is not a calibrated probability. The arrival-time baseline uses a 0.7-second reaction/projected-position assumption and 5 m/s travel speed; it does not integrate ball trajectories or reproduce Spearman. Source helpers expose all parameters.

## Accessible space and completion
Actual `accessible-space` package inference is used. Explicit first-half direction is inferred from persistent extreme player positions and must be checked against goalkeeper locations before changing matches. AS and DAS are computed on three selected frames. The coarse angular/ball-speed sampling supports workshop latency, not maximum-resolution analysis. A ball row with zero current velocity marks the launch position; the simulator supplies hypothetical pass velocities. Synthetic candidate pass endpoints are real teammate locations, not observed decisions. The supplied player-possession label determines the hypothetical passer; its correctness should still be checked against the clip. DAS is danger-weighted area, not a goal probability. AS/DAS and xC use different task defaults in the package; inspect parameter signatures rather than equating them.

## Graphs and value models
The original walkthrough radius graph is directed, with distance, dx/dy and same-team features. The standalone graph notebook first verifies derivatives on synthetic graphs, then trains an independent frame GNN on real SkillCorner tracking and evaluates unseen matches. Graph construction alone is not evidence of learned tactical relationships. Full provider EPV, run-threat and counterfactual models remain research references rather than reproduced systems. The standalone xG notebook trains location-only and contextual logistic models on real StatsBomb non-penalty shots, with identical match-grouped cross-validation folds. A model's prediction target, horizon, calibration and failure/turnover branches must be stated. Attention weights and before/after value differences are not causal attribution by themselves.

## xG comparison

`notebooks/methods/07_expected_goals.ipynb` uses StatsBomb Open Data from the men's World Cups 2018 and 2022, pinned at `4b73468fc5b0f1950f9f66fada70ad3a4f9327cb`. It excludes penalties/shootouts and requires an opposing goalkeeper and at least one visible defender, then evaluates both models on the same eligible cohort. Goal is taken directly from the shot-outcome label. Provider xG, shot endpoints and other post-shot features are excluded from model inputs.

The baseline uses distance and goal-opening angle. The contextual model adds goalkeeper distances to shooter/goal, goalkeeper offset from the goal-center line, visible defenders in the shot triangle, nearest visible defender distance and defenders within three coordinate units. Distances are in normalized StatsBomb coordinate units, not measured metres. The learner is the same L2-regularized logistic regression in both cases. Training-fold-only standardization, fixed regularization and the same five match-grouped folds isolate the feature comparison. Pooled out-of-fold ROC/AUC and paired match-bootstrap percentile intervals are accompanied by log loss and Brier score. The bootstrap does not refit models and does not quantify full training or transfer uncertainty. These are workshop models, not StatsBomb's production model. Data attribution and logo are included in the notebook and ROC figure.

## Illustrated event and structure methods
The nearest-player detector uses a 2 m zone and leaves other frames unassigned. It is not a validated possession or pass detector. A real ball path illustrates release/travel/reception cues around supplied event endpoints. Outfield role locations are team-centered medians split by possession. Ward clustering into three longitudinal lines is an exploratory baseline, not an asserted named formation. Phase figures display supplied SkillCorner intervals.

## Physical threshold intervals
SkillCorner's published explosive-acceleration description defines Time to HSR and Time to Sprint using the intervals from 9 to 20 and 9 to 25 km/h within qualifying explosive efforts. The slide plots use real continuous threshold crossings, selecting an observed sub-9 km/h sample as the illustrative start; they do not reconstruct official qualification, interpolation, Top 3 aggregation or production filtering. The acceleration slide differentiates scalar speed, not the magnitude of the acceleration vector.

## Fitted event-location xThreat
The external DataBallPy open-play grid is retained in resources/models with package-version and SHA-256 provenance and the upstream license. It is not fitted on this tracking sample. Its lookup convention uses a centered 106 x 68 m pitch attacking right. Our fixed-match example rotates first-half Brisbane coordinates into attack-right orientation and rescales longitudinal coordinates from 105 to 106 m. The arrow illustrates an observed ball displacement; no single completed pass or causal player contribution is asserted for that segment. This event-location xT is distinct from SkillCorner's supplied off-ball-run xthreat.

## Temporal graph inputs
The notebook builds one-second historical features only where the lag spans exactly ten native frame steps within the same player and period. Missing histories remain masked. Spatial edges are rebuilt at each frame; identity edges connect the same player only across consecutive observed frames and point forward. Recurrent, temporal-convolution, causal-attention and joint spatiotemporal models are explained separately with architecture schematics; no trained forecasts are supplied. The example's symmetric coordinate smoothing is suitable for offline retrospective analysis. For a live predictor, use causal preprocessing or account explicitly for smoothing latency before claiming leakage-free inference.

## Visual provenance

Physics-aware control, neural models and research projects use labeled architecture schematics where weights or task data are unavailable. The TacticAI illustration uses real corner-reception context, not original TacticAI training input or inference.

## Evaluation
Use held-out matches or sequences, not random neighboring frames. Validate preprocessing with clips; evaluate event detection with timing tolerances, probabilities with calibration/proper scores, and tactical labels with explicit taxonomies. Test sensitivity to missing/extrapolated positions, smoothing, thresholds, directions and physical parameters. The notebook is an educational analysis, not a production scouting or medical/load-monitoring system.
